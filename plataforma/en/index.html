De Olho na Pele demonstration

The SoLiz platform in three screens

The patient shows the skin lesion with a mobile phone. The nursing team at the health post checks it and decides where it goes. The doctor assesses it remotely and decides. Here you follow an invented case through the three screens, step by step.

This platform is only a guide for building your own. It can and should be adapted to your reality. Whether for a scientific study or just to use the panel, it can be taken apart for each situation. It is written in a language that is easy to rearrange. Make the most of it, and make your service a reference in skin cancer triage.

This is a research demonstration. The patient, the doctors and the municipality shown on the screens are invented. The screens on this page are images: nothing you do here is sent or stored. The program does not give a diagnosis and has not yet been used with patients.

Patient

app on the phone

The person shows the lesion from home, in plain words. There is no need to know the name of anything.

  • Points to the picture that looks most like the lesion, or says that none looks like it.
  • Tells the story of the lesion: whether it grew, changed, bleeds, itches.
  • Takes the photo with a dot sticker beside it, which serves as a measure, and points in the photo to which one is the lesion.
  • Receives a submission code and follows each step.

See the app, step by step

The patient app

15 steps. Tap an image to enlarge it.

  1. 1 of 15

    Registration and acceptance

    The person gives name, SUS card (the Brazilian public health card), CPF (the Brazilian tax number) and date of birth. Before going on, they read who will see their data and what for, and give their acceptance.

  2. 2 of 15

    What they want to show

    The person chooses what to show. In this demonstration only the spot or mole path is open. Someone with more than one lesion shows one at a time.

  3. 3 of 15

    The picture that looks like it

    The person looks at eight photos and taps the one that looks most like their lesion. They can also say that none looks like it. Two of the eight appear here.

  4. 4 of 15

    Questions for that picture

    Each picture brings its own questions. For a dark, round mole, the questions about shape and edge help little. The app asks others: whether it is the darkest of all, whether it appeared out of nowhere, whether it is the only one like that.

  5. 5 of 15

    The place on the body

    The person taps the drawing, at the place closest to where the lesion is. In this case, the upper back.

  6. 6 of 15

    The appearance, with examples

    Three questions about appearance: the halves, the edge and the colours. Each one comes with two example photos and accepts an answer in doubt, such as “I think they are the same”.

  7. 7 of 15

    The story of the lesion

    Only the person knows the story of the lesion: whether it grew, changed colour or shape, how long it has been there, whether it bleeds, itches or hurts. When they do not know, they answer “I don’t know”.

  8. 8 of 15

    The measure

    So that the size can be measured in the photo, the person sticks a yellow 12 mm dot sticker beside the lesion, the kind sold in stationery shops. They can also use an ordinary ruler or go on without a measure.

  9. 9 of 15

    The photo

    The app shows how the photo should look: the dot sticker beside the lesion, not touching it, close up, without zoom and in good light. In this demonstration the photo is simulated: an example photo appears in place of the person’s photo.

  10. 10 of 15

    Pointing in the photo

    There may be more than one similar thing in the photo. The person marks which one is their lesion with an arrow or a circle, and the marking goes along to the doctor.

  11. 11 of 15

    In their own words

    An optional space for the person to tell whatever they find important. If there is strong pain, bleeding or pus, the app tells them to go to the health post the same day, without waiting.

  12. 12 of 15

    Questions about the person

    They are asked only once and apply to everything the person shows: sun, sunburns, skin cancer in the family, moles on the body, smoking, drinking.

  13. 13 of 15

    The skin tone

    The person compares their skin with ten tones, looking at a part of the body that does not get sun. It is the Monk scale, which is open source.

  14. 14 of 15

    Done

    The submission receives a code. With it the health post confirms that the submission arrived intact, and the person follows three steps: sent, checked by the health post, assessed by the doctor. The app does not give a diagnosis.

  15. 15 of 15

    The notice arrives

    After the nursing team and the doctor have done their part, the notice from the health post appears in the app. If the lesion changes, the person taps “It changed, I want to tell you” and the case reopens with the nursing team.

Nursing

at the health post

Everything goes through the nursing team before it reaches the doctor.

  • Checks the person only once: whether the registration is genuine and whether they want to go ahead.
  • Gives each lesion a destination and releases the submission to the queue.
  • Receives the report back and notifies the patient. Communicates the referral, never the diagnosis.
  • Carries out what the doctor asked for: a new photo, a test, a biopsy.

See the nursing screen

The nursing screen

9 steps. Tap an image to enlarge it.

  1. 1 of 9

    What needs action

    The panel gathers what is waiting for the nursing team: people with a new case, reports that came back, biopsies to schedule, requests for a dermatoscope photo, referrals and claims. The numbers are invented.

  2. 2 of 9

    The submission arrives from the app

    The submission arrives with the same code the patient received, and the screen checks the log. The nursing team sees the person once, with the risk factors they reported. One person is one registration, and each lesion is a case.

  3. 3 of 9

    The two filters

    Before anything else, two checks per person. Is the registration genuine and from the municipality? Does the person want the assessment, knowing that it may lead to tests and a biopsy? The decision is the person’s.

  4. 4 of 9

    What the patient sent

    Each lesion comes with the note about the place on the body, the sentence the person wrote, the photo and the answers. The nursing team receives the same photo as the doctor. What it does not have are the doctor’s tools. In the demonstration it is the example photo the app showed, with the dot sticker beside the lesion.

  5. 5 of 9

    The destination of each lesion

    The nursing team chooses where each lesion goes. Here, the dermatologist queue. Keeping it with the nursing team is allowed only when the lesion has no sign and the photo has been checked. In this case the option appears blocked, because the spot has a sign.

  6. 6 of 9

    The submission goes to the queue

    Once the submission is released, the doctor takes the person and reports case by case. If a new lesion from the same person arrives, the nursing team decides whether to attach it to the submission or release a new one. A case already with the doctor is not touched.

  7. 7 of 9

    The report comes back

    The report comes back to the nursing team. What the doctor asked for becomes a task, with who asked, when and for which case. Here: a new photo of the same lesion in three months.

  8. 8 of 9

    Notifying the patient

    The nursing team notifies the patient through the app, in plain language. It communicates the referral. The diagnosis is communicated by the doctor.

  9. 9 of 9

    The timeline

    Everything that happened with the person stays in order, with the time. In the demonstration the steps fall within the same minute. In a real case they take days.

Doctor

assesses remotely

The doctor sees the whole case on one screen and decides.

  • Takes the next case in the queue. There is no choosing: the doctor receives the one with the highest priority.
  • Sees in separate blocks what the patient described, what the lesion went through and the person’s risk factors.
  • Decides the management or asks for what is missing: another photo, a test, a question to the patient.
  • The decision goes back to the nursing team, which carries it out.

See the doctor’s screen

The doctor’s screen

8 steps. Tap an image to enlarge it.

  1. 1 of 8

    The blind queue

    The doctor does not choose the case. The system hands over the highest-priority one that is waiting, so that nobody jumps the queue and no easy case goes ahead of a serious one.

  2. 2 of 8

    The case

    At the top, the note about the place on the body and where the case came from. In this demonstration the colour arrives green because the rule that proposes the colour is not yet written in the program. The screen tells the doctor: it is a proposal, the decision is theirs.

  3. 3 of 8

    The photo and the patient’s marking

    The photo arrives with the marking the patient made, showing which one is the lesion. The marking is a layer on top: it does not change the photo, and the doctor hides it at will, to look at and report on the clean image. If needed, the doctor asks for a dermatoscope photo. In the demonstration it is the example photo from the app.

  4. 4 of 8

    What the patient described

    In separate blocks: the appearance the patient pointed to and what the lesion went through. Each answer appears as it was given. The name of the picture pointed to is that of the reference photo the patient chose. It is not a diagnosis of the case.

  5. 5 of 8

    The person’s risk factors

    Collected once, they apply to all of the person’s cases. They stay in a separate block, without mixing with the answers about the lesion. The screen also warns that the check between the description and the photo is not yet done.

  6. 6 of 8

    The decision

    The doctor decides the management: reassure and advise, watch over time, recommend a biopsy or refer to another specialty. If something is missing, the doctor asks for it: a dermatoscope photo, a test, a task for the health post or a message to the patient.

  7. 7 of 8

    Watching over time

    In the demonstration, the management chosen was to watch over time. The health post calls the patient for a new photo of the same lesion in three months, and whoever takes the case later sees today’s photo and report side by side.

  8. 8 of 8

    The timeline of the case

    The case carries the submission code and the checked log. The doctor sees who did what before them.

The three screens linked

The three screens exchange a record of the case. Each step is written in a log of the submission, with a seal calculated from the previous line. The patient receives the submission code, and with it the health post confirms that the submission arrived intact.

The seal shows whether someone changed a line afterwards. It does not prevent anyone from changing it.

What this demonstration is not

  • It is not a health service, it is not a medical device and it does not give a diagnosis.
  • It has not been used with patients and is not meant for real data.
  • The photo is simulated. The app shows an example photo in place of the person’s photo, and that example photo is what reaches the nursing team and the doctor.
  • The program that measures the lesion in the photo is not connected to these screens.
  • The priority colour is not yet calculated. The rule is not written in the program, and the case that comes from the app is released as green.
  • The three screens talk to each other inside the same device. To work between devices, the health service’s server is still missing.
  • Only the spot or mole path is open.
  • The platform was built in Portuguese, for Brazil. The English on the screens and on this page was written by Claude (Anthropic, Fable model) from the author’s Portuguese, in November 2026. No clinician and no native speaker has reviewed it.
